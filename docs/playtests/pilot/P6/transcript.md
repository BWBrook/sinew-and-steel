
## G001 — Custodian

Rain needles the ruined tollhouse at Saltmere. Beneath it lies a crypt holding the village's lost burial register and a silver handbell. The villagers will pay for the register; a collector has offered a separate purse for the bell. You may bring back either, both, or neither.

A cold draught rises from a stone stair. Small muddy footprints lead down it. Behind a fall of masonry, a cracked wall opens onto darkness; the broken stones look loose enough to shift. Above you, the tollhouse hearth is cold but sheltered from the rain.

Bram, Iona, Mara, and Pip: what do you each do before the company goes below? You can examine the entrances, ready light or rope, discuss the commission, or take another approach. Tell me your method and who leads; no roll is called for yet.


## B001 — Bram Cinder

“I’ll take the front on the stairs, but let’s look at those muddy prints before we follow them. Pip, can you tell whether they’re coming up or going down? I’ll light a torch under the shelter of the hearth, then keep the rope ready in case the steps give way. The register is our first charge; we can decide about the bell when we know what guards it.”

## I001 — Iona Thistle

“I’ll ready my hooded lantern and look closely at those muddy prints before we descend. I want to know whether they lead into the crypt or come back out; I’m wary of following someone straight into a trap. Pip, would you take the lead on the stair once we’ve looked? I’ll keep the light behind you.”

## M001 — Mara Fen

“I’ll study those muddy prints before we disturb them—size, number, and whether any came back up. The burial register is our first duty to the village; I’d leave the bell alone until we know what it means here. Pip, if you’ll check the stair, I’ll lead beside you with my spear and holy symbol ready, and light a torch before we descend.”

## P001 — Pip Reed

“I’ll take first look at the muddy prints and the stair, keeping to the dry stone so I don’t spoil the tracks. I’ll sketch both entrances and check whether that cracked wall could serve as a way out if the stair turns dangerous. Bram, would you watch the loose masonry while I do it? I’d like us to agree how we’re getting back out before we go below.”

## G002 — Custodian

Pip sketches the two entrances while Bram steadies the loose stone. The cracked wall has a draught behind it and a gap that could be widened into a crawl with careful work. It may offer another route below, though you cannot yet see where it ends. Pulling stones out in haste could bring the rest down.

The muddy prints are from two small, shod travellers. Both went down the stair, and both came back up at least once. There is no sign of a struggle here. Bram and Mara light their torches; Iona's hooded lantern gives Pip enough light to check the first flight. Its steps hold, though the turn below is wet.

Pip leads you to the bend, with Bram ready behind and Mara beside him. A dry ledge opens across the stairwell. Two small figures watch your lights from it. One raises an empty oil flask and calls, “Easy there. Have you oil to trade? Take the long way if you want dry boots—and don't ring the bell while we're below.” They make no move to attack. What do you say or do?


## B002 — Bram Cinder

I keep my sword sheathed and my torch low. “We’re after the burial register, and we’ve no wish to trouble you. I’ve no oil to trade, but I can spare food or water. What happens if the bell rings, and where does the long way lead?” I stay between the strangers and the others while they answer.

## I002 — Iona Thistle

“I have oil to trade, though I need enough left to light our way out. What’s the long route, and why must the bell stay silent while you’re below? We’re here for the burial register; have you seen it?” I keep my lantern low and leave Pip room to speak.

## M002 — Mara Fen

“I lower my spear and hold the torch where they can see our faces. We have water and food to trade, though no oil. What happens if the bell rings while you’re below—and have you seen the burial register?”

## P002 — Pip Reed

“I’ve no oil, but I can spare a ration if you’ll tell us where the long way leads. We’re here for the burial register, and we mean to bring everyone back out safely. Why mustn’t the bell ring while you’re below?” I stay on the firm step and leave them room on the ledge.

## G003 — Custodian

The figure with the flask glances from Bram's sheathed sword to Iona's lantern. “Food we can find. Oil is harder.” His companion, perched well back from the edge, answers your question about the bell: “Last time it rang, the old keeper came walking. He listened at every door until we got out.”

They say the longer route stays dry and passes a room of old papers. They saw a locked niche there, but did not open it and cannot promise the register is inside. The shorter route crosses a flooded floor. Neither route requires you to trust them as guides.

The flask-holder turns to Iona. “Half your oil flask for directions to the dry passage and a warning about its doors. Keep the rest for your lamp. We'll stay clear of your way, if you leave that bell quiet while we're down here.” Iona, do you accept those terms, make another offer, or keep your oil?


## I003 — Iona Thistle

“I accept. Half my flask for the dry passage and the warning about its doors.” I pour the oil into their flask, keeping the rest for our lantern. “I won’t ring the bell while you’re below. Friends, are we agreed?”

## G004 — Custodian

Iona pours half her oil into the offered flask and keeps the rest. The speaker gives his name as Nib; his companion is Cress. Nib points down the stair. “At the fork, take the left arch with three cuts in its lintel. That's the dry way. Its oak door opens toward you, but the bottom sticks. Lift the latch and ease it free; shoving it makes a racket. The papers are beyond it. There's another little iron-bound door in that room—we found it locked.”

Cress adds, “The right-hand way goes over water. We didn't cross it.” They back along their ledge, leaving the stair clear. Nib watches the other three of you. “Your friend gave her word about the bell. Are the rest of you giving yours?”

Below the bend, you can see the fork and its three-cut lintel. Bram, Mara, and Pip: answer Nib if you wish. All four of you: do you take the dry arch, try the flooded way, return to the cracked wall, or do something else first?


## B004 — Bram Cinder

“You have my word, Nib: I won’t ring the bell while you’re below.” I take the dry arch with the three-cut lintel, staying ahead of Iona’s light. At the oak door I’ll hold the bottom steady while Pip lifts the latch and eases it open; I want us through quietly and together.

## I004 — Iona Thistle

“I gave my word, Nib, and I mean to keep it. I favour the dry arch and the room of papers.” I hold the lantern on the three-cut lintel and the floor ahead so Pip can check our footing, then follow behind him to the oak door.
