## Verdicts

**No crisis occurred.** Shadow starts at 0, gains 1 at event 46, and ends at 1; no crisis event, reset, or target exists. The 0–5 party fuse calls for a crisis only at 5 (`rules/core/custodians_almanac.md:58-60,80-85`; `skins/clanfire.md:96-107`). There is therefore no crisis ruling or crisis target to grade.

**Wrong — late milestone, with a beat-count caveat.** The fourth recorded perilous beat closes at beat 5/event 43 (C9), yet the sole award is after beat 10/events 64–67 (C16). The operative rule awards every 3–4 perilous beats (`rules/core/custodians_almanac.md:121-131`; `rules/quickstart.md:85-87`). The close award correctly gives each character 2 points, a boon and a full pool, but its timing denies the mid-session refill. Seven *logged* perilous beats would suggest a second award. That numerical conclusion is unsafe because the Custodian split the same wolf fight between beats 2 and 3 (events 22 and 34, C5–C7), contrary to “a fight is one beat” (`rules/core/custodians_almanac.md:190,197-201`). Counting that fight once gives six perilous scenes, compatible with one total award: C9/event 43 is then the third perilous scene and C12/event 54 the fourth, so the first award belongs within that C9–C12 window. The report's “two rounds” is also contradicted by the log: one `combat_start` at event 17, one round with wolf, Grak and Tarra each acting, then `combat_end` at event 33; no second-round event. Correct the descriptive count in subsequent analysis, without silently rewriting P1's historical JSONL.

**Judgement call — one Shadow for the Ysre contact.** Grak's first parley (C10) adds +1 at event 46. Tarra's later trade with the same group (C11/event 49) adds none. Clanfire lists “parley with Sapiens” as a +1 source but does not define whether a continuous negotiation is charged once or for each separate test (`skins/clanfire.md:96-109`). Treating ridge contact and its immediate trade as one parley is defensible. The elders' hearing (C12) is with the clan, so does not itself invoke the Sapiens parley trigger. The failed-risky-rite costs in C13 and C15 were declared; both dice were rescued before settlement, so no failure Shadow was due (`rules/scenarios/clanfire_emberfall_hidden.md:19-22`).

**Correct — both Pack Learns ticks can occur without a new roll.** Grak's natural 20 leaves him scented/heard and ticks 0→1 (C2/event 6). The crowded cave, meat scent and circling wolves later tick 1→2 during Grak's watch (C14/event 58). The scenario specifically ticks this clock when wolves smell blood, spot weakness or find an easy path (`rules/scenarios/clanfire_emberfall_hidden.md:24-27`); clocks may tick as time passes or fiction advances without dice (`rules/core/custodians_almanac.md:25-36,105-110`). The second tick is a discretionary threat escalation, not a fabricated roll. Hunger 0→1 for giving away the remaining calf share (C12/event 51) likewise fits food lost (`rules/scenarios/clanfire_emberfall_hidden.md:24-25`). Neither clock fills, so no clock-triggered consequence was owed.

**Judgement call — wolf first in combat.** C4 states before the calf roll that a loss starts a fight with the wolf attacking first. The loss occurs (events 14–16), and `combat_start` event 17 records `order: [pack, clan]` with no initiative dice. The rule permits fictional circumstances to settle first action; otherwise both sides roll initiative (`rules/core/adventurers_manual.md:221-225`). A lunging wolf after the agreed failed approach plausibly settles it, although the rule does not say that stating conditional stakes alone substitutes for initiative. C5–C7 and events 18–33 show wolf, Grak, then Tarra; no second attack or extra combat action is apparent. The log and final tracker expose `acted` and order, but the preserved evidence lacks a verbatim command sequence for every combat-turn call, so exact CLI turn enforcement is not independently auditable.

**Correct — good-care rest.** C8/event 35 restores Grak from Stamina 3→5, and events 36–37 restore one Instinct bead each to Grak and Tarra. The fiction provides fire, herb paste, binding, care and a pause. Clanfire grants 2 Stamina for herbs/patient care and one bead at a hearth rest (`skins/clanfire.md:30-36,55-59`); the core allows good care instead of the short rest's 1 Stamina, once per pause (`rules/core/adventurers_manual.md:178-194`). The final Grak sheet's Stamina 5/7 agrees. The empty character-sheet inventories mean the actual herbs and other gear are established by the packet, handout and narration, not carried in the final sheet.

**Correct — deferred settlements, with an early procedure deviation.** The unusual choices are C4/P4 (Tarra declines a 10-bead rescue of the calf contest), C6/P6 (Grak spends 2 after seeing 9 versus 18, raising axe margin from 3 to 5 and damage from 2 to 3), C13/P13 (Tarra spends 2 to move 16→14), and C15/P15 (Tarra spends 3 to move 17→14). Raw dice in events 14, 23, 55 and 60 remain unchanged in the corresponding settle events 15–16, 24–27, 56–57 and 61–62; natural 1/20 locks and nudge/damage calculations agree with `rules/core/adventurers_manual.md:164-171,221-242` and `skins/clanfire.md:28,53`. These calls do not establish that Tarra's nine Spirit rolls out of 14 PC rolls are a rules error; they flag a spotlight pattern for later runs. The late Luck spending immediately before refill is mechanically legal and may be strategic, but a single run cannot establish balance.

## Record and access checks

The final saved checkpoint (`final/p1_clanfire/state/checkpoints/last.md`) matches the C16 public body byte-for-byte after the transcript heading and surrounding separator are removed. Only `last.md`/`last.yaml` survive in the frozen final state; earlier exact Custodian replies cannot be compared to prior checkpoint versions. The log records mechanics, not public text. `summary.json` is consistent with the log's 10 beat events, seven marked perilous, 14 finalized PC rolls, one Shadow gain, no crisis, one milestone pair and session end; its count inherits the fight-splitting problem above. The final checkpoint also says `SESSION CLOSED`, and event 68 records session end. The ignored `frozen_hidden_scenario.md` is byte-for-byte identical to the published Emberfall hidden scenario in this checkout; an independent file-write trace of when it was frozen is unavailable.

I found no hidden scenario twist, Pack Learns clock name, Storm clock value, private NPC motive or crisis-table result in the public transcript or the packet search. C13 *does* tell Tarra the Shadow step-2 and step-4 levers from `skins/clanfire.md:96-105`, a Custodian-facing section omitted from the player packets. Those levers are not the forbidden crisis table and the numerical Shadow track was openly shown, so classify this as **judgement call / packet-boundary disclosure**, not a demonstrated hidden-scenario leak. Decide before the programme whether public Shadow includes its step effects; the protocol's player-packet rule currently excludes Custodian-facing skin lines (`docs/playtests/pilot-protocol.md`, Access; `skins/clanfire.md:92-107`).

Player access was instructed, not enforced. The manifest says each player made exactly one Read call for a packet file, contrary to the protocol's literal “receive their packet as text”/“do not use tools”; it also says there were no later tool calls. The frozen packets are present and contain player-facing material. There is no player tool-call transcript or operating-system access log in the auditor's evidence, so the exact one-call count and absence of further browsing are **not assessable independently**. The observed public messages show no use of hidden information; that supports a narrower no-observed-leak claim, never hard isolation (`docs/playtests/pilot-protocol.md`, Access and Records).

Priority for the programme: keep the historical P1 records intact, but annotate analyses with the one-round/one-fight beat correction; make milestone timing explicit in the Custodian brief; preserve a full checkpoint and tool/command trace if exact reply matching, turn enforcement and player access are intended audit claims. Treat the Shadow scope and public step-effect boundary as decisions to state prospectively, not conclusions forced by this one session.
