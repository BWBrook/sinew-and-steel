# The Adventurer

Hazardry asks something simple of you as a player: decide what your character would do, and commit to it.

Think as this person would, right now, with their courage, habits, pride, fear, and bad information.

The rules are short on purpose. They catch you when the stakes matter, then get out of the way.

## Play the person, not the puzzle

You can tune a character in Hazardry, but build optimisation, rules lawyering, and fishing for a convenient check flatten play. Play instead to find out what your character becomes, failures included.

## Keep the pace

Hazardry plays best when the action feels like it is happening in real time. Help keep that pace:

- Be ready to act when the spotlight lands on you.
- Choose an approach quickly, even if it isn’t perfect.
- Asking questions and thinking aloud are fine. If you stall in the fiction, though, treat it as real: time passes, attention shifts, danger closes.

## Declare intent, method, risk

The most useful declarations in Hazardry sound like this:

- **I want** ___ (intent)
- **by doing** ___ (method)
- **and I’m worried** ___ (risk)

Example:

> “I want the guard looking the other way, so I distract him with a joke and a quick question, but I’m worried he might recognise me.”

Give the Custodian something vivid and specific, and you will often get a cleaner ruling in return: a narrative outcome, Advantage, or a failure cost that feels fair.

## Commit before the future is certain

Before you roll, ask what your character could reasonably know about the stakes. The Custodian tells you the likely cost of failure, though hidden causes and later complications can still surprise you. Once the stakes and your method are clear, commit to the attempt.

Useful things to ask:

- In-fiction details: “How thick is the ice?” “Do I smell smoke?”
- What a failed attempt could cost in time, harm, resources, or exposure.

Then accept that the corner may still hide a surprise, and trust the Custodian to adjudicate in good faith.

## Let stats follow approach

By default, the Custodian calls the stat. You may propose an alternative if it fits your approach better; give a one-line justification.

If there is disagreement, the Custodian decides, briefly and calmly. A tense beat goes flat once it becomes a courtroom.

## Some actions resolve in the fiction

You can earn roll-free outcomes by removing the uncertainty: good prep, useful tools, strong positioning, or a clever plan that leaves no real chance of failure. And when failure would cost nothing interesting, there is nothing to roll for.

## Spend Luck like a storyteller

Spending Luck trades future safety for success now. Two good instincts pull against each other:

- Spend early to seize momentum (the roll that defines the scene).
- Save for existential risk (the roll that might end the adventure).

When you nudge a die, narrate it. Say what it looks like: a lucky foothold, a surge of courage, a remembered detail, a coincidence that feels like destiny, or (in some skins) a price paid to something listening.

## When you fail

Failure in Hazardry can mean a hard stop, a complication, progress at a cost, or success with collateral damage. Whatever form it takes, it changes the situation.

Your character wants to survive. Play that, and let the failures land.

## Fighting is a kind of scene

Combat is dangerous, and “victory” means what the fiction says it means. NPCs and monsters usually value their own lives, and the Custodian plays those motives through the scene.

The game rewards you for getting through perilous beats, not for winning fights. Useful instincts:

- Avoid fair fights; make them unfair in your favour.
- Retreat freely. Surviving counts as a win.
- Threats, bargains, bribes, ambushes, and simply not being there all work.
- Driving something off can be as decisive as killing it.

## Table habits that help

- Share the spotlight; pull another character into your plan.
- Say openly what you spend or risk (Luck, Stamina, Pressure).
- Accept consequences and play them out.

## A vignette: choosing danger on purpose (Iron & Ruin)

In the Iron & Ruin skin, sorcery is a bargain whose price keeps climbing.

> The cultists are closing and the iron gate won’t budge.<br>
> “You can try to lift it,” the Custodian says, “but not before they reach you.”<br>
> The sorceress smiles. “Then I *Rend* it.”<br>
> “That’s a Wrack-tier spell. Test Will, no Fortune nudges. Success costs a point of Doom; failure costs two and a backlash.”<br>
> She speaks the forbidden name anyway.<br>
> The gate screams, warps, and splits. The party bolts through as the air turns cold.<br>
> She marks +1 Doom and laughs, wiping a trickle of blood from her lip.
